# China Metro — Beijing & Shanghai

China's metro systems are among the best in the world — clean, frequent, safe, and inexpensive (¥3-10 per trip, about $0.40-$1.40). Here's what you need:

## Payment Methods

| Method | How | Best For |
|--------|-----|----------|
| Alipay/WeChat Pay | Scan QR code at gate | Regular travelers |
| Single-journey ticket | Buy from machine | First-time, occasional |
| Transport card | Tap card | Long stays |

## Metro Names

| City | Lines | Color of Line Signs | Fare Range |
|------|-------|---------------------|------------|
| Beijing | 20+ | White text on colored background | ¥3-10 |
| Shanghai | 18+ | Colored lines | ¥3-12 |

## Common Station Signs to Recognize

| Chinese | English | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------|
| 进站 | Jìn zhàn | Entering the paid area |
| 出站 | Chū zhàn | Exiting / Exit |
| 换乘 | Huàn chéng | Transfer to another line |
| 出口 | Chū kǒu | Exit (followed by letter: A, B, C...) |
| 卫生间 | Wèi shēng jiān | Restroom |
| 客服中心 | Kè fú zhōng xīn | Customer service center |

## Pro Tips

1. **Stand right, walk left** on escalators — same as in Singapore/HK
2. **Mornings (7-9am) and evenings (5-7pm)** are crowded — pack light and know your stops
3. **Airport lines** connect directly to Beijing Capital, Beijing Daxing, and Shanghai Pudong airports
4. **Don't eat on trains** — officially not allowed, though some people do
