## What AMap is, and why it matters here

AMap is the international name for Gaode Maps (高德地图), the map and navigation app Chinese users actually navigate with. If you arrive expecting the map app you use at home to behave normally, you will spend your first day fighting it. AMap is the practical substitute, and its real value is not the map tiles — it is that every place has a **Chinese name attached**, which is the single most useful thing you can extract from a phone in China.

Our [internet guide](/guides/get-internet-in-china) covers which services are reachable without a VPN. This guide does not repeat that.

## What the announcement actually confirms

Everything in this section comes from AMap's own launch announcement, reported by People's Daily Online on 14 July 2025, plus the App Store listing.

- AMap introduced an **English-language map designed for overseas users** earlier in 2025, and on **9 July 2025** added a multilingual version covering 14 further languages.
- The announcement states the multilingual version is downloadable from both the **App Store and Google Play**.
- It has a **localised interface and localised place information**, including names and descriptions of attractions, restaurants and **hotels**.
- Overseas users get the **same driving, walking, cycling and public transport navigation** as local users.
- It includes an **English-language ride-hailing function** that books across multiple platforms in one tap, with pickup details and fare estimates.

The App Store listing confirms the app is published as **AMap Global** by **AutoNavi Information Technology Co. Ltd.**, in the Navigation category.

## What this guide does not confirm

Be clear about the boundary, because these are exactly the claims that circulate in blogs and are not established here:

- **Offline maps.** This guide does not claim you can download city maps and navigate without a connection. That ability is not confirmed by the sources above.
- **Registration with a foreign phone number.** The app may ask you to sign in, and it may refuse a non-Chinese number. Do not build your plan around being logged in.
- **Availability in every store or country.** The announcement refers to the App Store and Google Play at launch time; it makes no promise about your specific regional store today.
- **That AMap replaces other options.** It does not. Apple Maps and Baidu Maps remain alternatives, and a paper copy of your Chinese address remains the fallback that works when nothing else does.

If a step in this guide describes an on-screen control, treat it as the shape of the flow rather than a pixel-exact guarantee — app layouts change between versions.

## Finding a place and reading its Chinese name

The workflow is three moves: **search in English → open the place → copy the Chinese string**.

Search is the easy part; the app is localised for overseas users. The step people skip is the third one, and it is the one that pays off. Ordering a ride, asking directions, or typing into a building's front desk system all become trivial once you are holding the characters instead of the translation.

Save that string to a note, or screenshot the place card, **before** you need it. Data at street level is often worse than it was in your hotel room, and the moment you most need the address is the moment you are least able to look it up.

## Giving a driver something to point at

A driver cannot use an English hotel name, and a spoken approximation is worse. Two things travel well:

1. The **Chinese name and address**, as characters, on your screen or on paper.
2. A **nearby landmark** the driver will recognise, which you can usually get from the same place card.

If your ride is booked in an app, paste the Chinese name into the app's destination or chat field rather than typing English. If you are in a street taxi, hand over the phone. If the driver still cannot find the place, the [address troubleshooting guide](/problems/cant-find-address) has the recovery paths.

## Hailing a ride from inside the map

The international version bundles ride-hailing, which means you can go from "I want to go here" to "a car is coming" without switching apps. It shows pickup information and a fare estimate.

If you would rather use a dedicated app with a more familiar flow, use [DiDi](/guides/use-didi-in-china) — but still paste the Chinese address rather than typing English.

## Using it alongside the metro

The map gets you to the station entrance and tells you which exit to leave from. Gate procedures, tickets, and payment at the gate are a different problem, covered in the [metro guide](/guides/use-metro-in-china). If a gate rejects your payment method, see [metro payment troubleshooting](/problems/metro-payment-failed).

## If AMap will not install, open, or search

Work down this list:

1. **Install at home.** Availability and store access are far easier to solve before you fly.
2. **Try the alternative map apps** already on your phone — Apple Maps on iOS, Baidu Maps as another Chinese option.
3. **Go analog.** Ask your hotel front desk to write the Chinese address and a nearby landmark on paper. Keep it in your pocket, not in the app.
4. **Use your booking confirmation.** It usually contains the hotel's Chinese name and address already.
5. **Ask a person.** Hotel staff, airport information desks, and metro staff can point you at a taxi queue or write directions.

None of these require a working account, a login, or a data connection.
