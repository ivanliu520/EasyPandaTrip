## Why the Chinese address matters more than the English one

A hotel's English name is a label for you, not a destination for anyone else. The Beijing municipal government's arrival guidance puts the practical point plainly: most taxi drivers do not speak English, so visitors should print their destination's address in Chinese before arrival. The same page tells airport shuttle passengers to have their Chinese address ready because staff are unlikely to speak English.

That is the entire argument for this guide. Everything below is about getting that string right, keeping it usable, and handing it over cleanly.

If you are troubleshooting a ride that has already gone wrong, the [address troubleshooting page](/problems/cant-find-address) is the better starting point — this guide is about preparing.

## Where the Chinese text comes from

The first place to look is your own booking confirmation. Where the provider included the Chinese property name and address, that text is authoritative in the only sense that matters: it came from the property rather than from a translation tool.

Two rules:

- **Copy, do not retype.** Character-level typos are invisible to you and fatal to a search.
- **Do not translate it yourself.** A machine translation of an address is a plausible-looking string that may point at a different building. Get the Chinese form from the property or from a map app, not from a translator.

## Check the branch before you trust the address

Chain hotels routinely operate several properties in one city under nearly identical names. An address that is correct for the brand can still be wrong for your booking.

Read for the distinguishing detail — a road name, a district, a building or mall name — and compare it against the branch details on your confirmation. If two candidates are too close to call, call the property on the number in your confirmation and ask. That call costs a minute before you fly and saves an hour at midnight.

## Making it survive a dead phone

Carry the address in three forms, because each fails in a different way:

| Form | Fails when | Why keep it |
|---|---|---|
| Note on your phone | Battery dies, app won't open | Easy to copy from, easy to paste |
| Screenshot | Screen too dim, image cropped | Readable without any app or connection |
| Paper | Gets lost or wet | Needs no battery, no signal, no permission |

Paper is the one people skip and the one that works when nothing else does. Write the address and the property's local phone number on the same slip.

One caution: this guide does not claim that any map app can navigate without a connection. A screenshot is readable offline because it is an image, not because a map has been cached.

## Showing it instead of saying it

Hand the phone or the paper over. Do not read the characters aloud — you will not pronounce them correctly, and pronunciation is not what the other person needs.

If you are in a street taxi, showing the address at the start of the trip is the whole transaction. If the driver hesitates, the next thing to offer is the hotel's local phone number, which lets the driver settle the question with the front desk.

## Using it in an app

Paste the Chinese string into your map or ride-hailing app rather than typing an English hotel name. English input can match the wrong property; a pasted Chinese string cannot be silently mistranslated.

Two workflows already documented on this site, which this guide deliberately does not repeat:

- Finding a place in English and reading its Chinese name in the map app: [AMap guide](/guides/use-amap-maps-in-china).
- Booking a ride and pasting the destination: [DiDi guide](/guides/use-didi-in-china).

## When only an English address exists

This is common, and it is not a crisis. Work in this order:

1. **Ask the property.** Message or call the hotel and request the address in Chinese. They are the authoritative source.
2. **Ask at check-in.** Front desk staff can write the address down, and a handwritten slip is as good as a printout.
3. **Use a map app** to find the place in English and copy the Chinese name it displays — see the [AMap guide](/guides/use-amap-maps-in-china).
4. **Travel prepared to improvise**: English address plus the hotel's local phone number gives a driver or a bystander enough to solve it.

None of these require you to speak Chinese. All of them require that you do not invent characters.

## What this guide does not claim

- That every booking confirmation contains Chinese text. Check yours; if it is missing, use the steps above.
- That any map app works offline, or that a downloaded map will be available when your connection is not.
- Any specific translation of a hotel name, address, or phone number. Those come from the property, not from this page.
- That hotel staff or drivers will speak English. The source guidance assumes the opposite, which is why the address is on paper.
