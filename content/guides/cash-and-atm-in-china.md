## Why arrange cash at all

China is heavily cashless, and mobile payment is the normal way to pay. But "usually cashless" is not "always working". The realistic failure cases are mundane: a phone that has run out of battery, a payment-app registration that has not cleared yet, or a card that a particular machine will not read. A small amount of yuan turns each of those from a blocked trip into an inconvenience.

Treat cash as a backup layer under your mobile payment method, not as your primary plan. The [Alipay guide](/guides/use-alipay-china) and the [WeChat Pay guide](/guides/use-wechat-pay-china) cover the primary path.

## What Bank of China actually documents

Everything in this section is specific to **Bank of China**, and comes from its English ATM page and its guide for short-term international visitors. Other Chinese banks publish their own terms, which this guide does not cover.

### Cards and ATMs

Bank of China states that its ATMs provide around-the-clock support for cash withdrawals and balance enquiries using major international bank cards issued outside China — UnionPay, Visa, Mastercard, JCB and American Express. It also notes that the specific services available, and the fees for withdrawals and enquiries, are determined by the overseas issuing bank.

Bank of China's visitor guide adds that at its merchants — airports, hotels, stores, supermarkets, restaurants and entertainment venues — cards carrying the UnionPay, Visa or Mastercard logo are accepted where those logos are displayed at the checkout counter, and that you can ask the cashier if a logo is not shown.

### The CNY 3,000 per-transaction limit

Bank of China documents a **single-transaction limit of CNY 3,000** for ATM cash withdrawals using foreign cards within China. This is a per-withdrawal cap at Bank of China, not a daily allowance and not a nationwide rule. If you need more cash than that, make a second withdrawal.

### Fees are set by your bank

Bank of China does not set the fee you pay for using your foreign card — it says plainly that fees for cash withdrawals and enquiries are determined by the overseas issuing bank. That is why this guide gives no figures: any number would be wrong for most readers and would age badly. Check your own bank's overseas withdrawal terms, including any fixed fee per withdrawal, before you travel.

## If the ATM keeps your card

Bank of China's documented procedure, from its visitor guide:

1. Keep the printed slip the machine produces.
2. Contact the staff of the institution that manages the ATM, or call Bank of China's customer service centre on **95566**, to confirm the time and process for collecting the card.
3. Collect the card within the period Bank of China prescribes for retained cards.

The slip matters because it is the record you will be asked to produce. If the branch is shut when it happens, call 95566 before you leave the area rather than coming back the next day without having confirmed anything.

## Exchanging banknotes instead of withdrawing

If an ATM will not cooperate, banknote exchange is the documented alternative:

- **Bank of China outlets** exchange up to 38 foreign currencies, including US dollar, euro, pound sterling, Japanese yen, Hong Kong dollar and others. For small amounts you can use the service by presenting a passport or other valid ID, without opening an account.
- **Authorised exchange counters** — Bank of China has authorised more than 1,300 hotel reception counters and airport exchange counters. You present valid ID and foreign banknotes, with the amount limited to **CNY 10,000 per person per day** and **CNY 50,000 per year**.
- **Self-service exchange machines** at airports, ports and hotels support up to 20 foreign currencies and exchange foreign banknotes directly into RMB banknotes.

The per-day and per-year figures above are the counter-exchange limits Bank of China publishes. They are a different thing from the ATM per-transaction cap, and you should not treat them as interchangeable.

## What the rules say about accepting cash

Chinese policy is explicitly protective of cash. In the 2024 guideline on payment services for foreign visitors and elderly residents, the authorities encouraged cash acceptance across transport, shopping, catering and entertainment businesses, and the central bank stated that instances of vendors rejecting cash would be investigated and penalised, with the renminbi's status as legal tender guaranteed. The same guideline also pushed to expand acceptance of overseas bank cards at hotels, hospitals, tourist attractions and major business districts; a central bank official cited a 90% international-card acceptance rate among key businesses in Shanghai and Zhejiang, as of March 2024.

Two cautions on reading that:

- That 90% figure covers **two regions in 2024**. It is not a national figure and not a current one.
- A policy requirement is not the same as uniform behaviour at every counter. Keep a second payment method regardless.

## Where cash and cards still work in practice

Concrete, documented examples rather than general claims. The Beijing municipal government's arrival guidance describes:

- Airport metro ticket machines taking WeChat Pay and Alipay, and manned counters taking **international cards and CNY cash**.
- Airport shuttle bus booths accepting **international cards**.
- A "tap to ride" option on the metro using Visa or Mastercard.

Use those as your known-good points during the first hours after landing, when you most need a working option. The [metro guide](/guides/use-metro-in-china) covers gate procedures and failures.

## Your fallback chain

Work down this list rather than improvising at the machine:

1. ATM at your own bank's partner network, or an ATM displaying your card's logo.
2. A second ATM at a different bank.
3. Bank branch or authorised exchange counter, passport in hand.
4. Mobile payment (Alipay or WeChat Pay) — the primary path, and the one to have ready before you fly.
5. Hotel front desk or airport information desk, to point you at the nearest open exchange option.

## What this guide does not claim

- That every ATM in China accepts every foreign card. Only that Bank of China documents support for the five networks above at its own ATMs.
- Any specific fee, exchange rate, or daily withdrawal limit. Those are set by your issuing bank.
- That every merchant accepts cash, cards, or mobile payment. The 2024 acceptance figure cited above is limited to key businesses in two regions.
