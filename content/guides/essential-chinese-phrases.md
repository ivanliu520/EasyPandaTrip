## How to use this page

Every phrase below is written in **simplified characters** with **tone-marked pinyin**. The tone marks are part of the spelling, not decoration: the same syllable with a different mark is a different word. Read the mark on each vowel and keep it.

Two rules that matter more than pronunciation:

- **Show before you speak.** If you cannot produce a tone reliably, hold up the characters. Reading them aloud badly is worse than pointing at them.
- **One phrase, one purpose.** These are not sentences you can rearrange. Each line is chosen for the situation named beside it.

This is a short survival set, not a phrasebook, and it does not replace a translation app. The [cant-understand-chinese problem page](/problems/cant-understand-chinese) covers translation tools and what to do when neither your Chinese nor an app is working.

## Greetings and politeness

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hello | 你好 | Nǐ hǎo | Entering a shop, starting any interaction |
| Thank you | 谢谢 | Xièxie | After any help, however small |
| Excuse me / may I ask | 请问 | Qǐngwèn | In front of a question to a stranger |
| Sorry | 对不起 | Duìbuqǐ | Bumping into someone, interrupting |

请问 is the highest-value phrase on this page. It converts a bare question into a polite one, and it is the correct opener for the price and restroom lines below.

`你好吗？` ("how are you?") is deliberately **not** included. The phrasebook sources list it, but nothing reviewed here establishes how it lands with a stranger, and it is not needed to open an interaction. 你好 does that job.

The phrasebook source gives 对不起 with two readings, `Duìbùqǐ` and `Duìbuqǐ` — the difference is whether the second syllable carries a fourth tone or is neutral. This page uses `Duìbuqǐ`.

## Asking whether someone speaks English

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do you speak English? | 你会说英语吗？ | Nǐ huì shuō Yīngyǔ ma? | Front desk, station counter, any service counter |
| I can't speak Chinese | 我不会说汉语。 | Wǒ bú huì shuō hànyǔ | Explaining the situation in one sentence |
| I don't understand (what I heard) | 我听不懂。 | Wǒ tīng bu dǒng | The other person has started talking |

The `吗` at the end is the question marker; leave it in. 我听不懂 is narrower and more accurate than the broader `我不懂` that appears on some pages: it says specifically that you did not understand what was said.

## Asking for help

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| I need your help | 我需要你的帮助。 | Wǒ xūyào nǐ de bāngzhù | Approaching a bystander or staff member |
| Where is the restroom? | 洗手间在哪里？ | Xǐshǒujiān zài nǎlǐ? | Malls, stations, restaurants |
| Where is the hospital? | 医院在哪里？ | Yīyuàn zài nǎlǐ? | Illness or injury away from your hotel |

**To be taken somewhere, show the written Chinese address rather than say it.** Save your destination's Chinese name and address before you leave — it is on your booking confirmation, and the [hotel address guide](/guides/get-hotel-address-in-chinese) walks through getting it — then hand the screen to the driver. A written address does not depend on your tones, and it needs no word like "here" to be understood.

If you are already lost rather than about to travel, the [address troubleshooting page](/problems/cant-find-address) has its own four-phrase table for that situation; this page does not repeat it.

## Paying and asking prices

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| How much? | 多少钱？ | Duōshao qián? | Markets, small shops, anywhere without a printed price |
| Too expensive | 太贵了 | Tài guì le | Markets and stalls, when a price is negotiable |
| Can I pay by card? | 可以刷卡吗？ | Kěyǐ shuākǎ ma? | Counter payment |
| Can I use Alipay? | 可以用支付宝吗？ | Kěyǐ yòng Zhīfùbǎo ma? | Counter payment |
| I don't want it | 我不要！ | Wǒ bú yào! | Declining persistent sellers |

On the pinyin of 多少钱: CC-CEDICT lists 多少 as `duōshao` for the meaning "how much?", while another dictionary entry records `duōshǎo`. Both readings are attested; this page uses `Duōshao qián?`, consistent with the rest of this site. The tone of `qián` is not in dispute.

Editor's note, not a sourced claim: 太贵了 is for places where haggling is normal. Said in a fixed-price shop it reads as a complaint rather than an opening offer. Payment itself is covered in the [Alipay guide](/guides/use-alipay-china) and the [WeChat Pay guide](/guides/use-wechat-pay-china).

可以刷卡吗？ and 可以用支付宝吗？ both use the 可以…吗 frame, which asks whether you **may** use that method. They are permission questions, not a request for the shop's full list of accepted payment methods — a "no" answers about that method alone.

## Food and dietary needs

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Could I see the menu, please | 请给我菜单。 | Qǐng gěi wǒ càidān | After sitting down |
| Do you have an English menu? | 有英文菜单吗？ | Yǒu Yīngwén càidān ma? | Before ordering, at the table |
| I don't eat spicy food | 我不吃辣。 | Wǒ bù chī là | Ordering, as a standing restriction |

请给我菜单 and 我不吃辣 are dictionary example sentences, not assembled by hand. 有英文菜单吗？ is built from checked words plus the same `吗` question pattern.

`不要辣` ("no chili") is **not** included: its grammatical completeness and register were not established by any source reviewed here, so 我不吃辣 is the safer line for the same purpose.

## Emergencies

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Help! | 救命！ | Jiùmìng! | Immediate danger |
| Police! | 警察！ | Jǐngchá! | Summoning help |
| I'll call the police | 我要叫警察了。 | Wǒ yào jiào jǐngchá le | Deterring a situation |

Dial **110** for the police. These three lines are for the seconds before a phone is in your hand, not a substitute for the number.

## Related guides and limitations

**What this page does not claim:**

- That these phrases will be understood. Tones and context decide that, and neither is guaranteed by a table.
- That the pinyin here is the only accepted reading. Where the sources on this page disagree — 多少钱 and 对不起 — the difference is stated above rather than hidden.
- That saying a phrase replaces a translation app, a map, or a written address. For anything with a specific destination or price attached, show the object.

**How each phrase was checked.** Three kinds of evidence appear above, and they are not equally strong:

| Basis | Phrases | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Complete-phrase evidence | 你好, 谢谢, 请问, 对不起, 你会说英语吗？, 我不会说汉语。, 我听不懂。, 我需要你的帮助。, 洗手间在哪里？, 多少钱？, 我不要！, 请给我菜单。, 我不吃辣。, 救命！, 警察！, 我要叫警察了。 | A source presents the complete phrase with its pinyin and meaning, or as an example sentence |
| Word-level evidence | 太贵了, 可以刷卡吗？, 可以用支付宝吗？, 有英文菜单吗？ | Each word is a dictionary entry; the sentence is assembled here and is not itself attested |
| Pattern-level evidence | 医院在哪里？ | A comparable sentence appears in a source — 洗手间在哪里？ and 厕所在哪里？ — but this exact sentence does not |

Advice that is a judgment rather than a finding is labelled as such: the haggling note under 太贵了, and the instruction to show an address instead of saying it, are practical reasoning, not dictionary entries.

The two reference works are also not fully independent of each other: MDBG publishes CC-CEDICT, and Wiktionary's Chinese entries draw on the same underlying data, so agreement between them confirms spelling and reading more than real-world usage. That is why this guide stays `draft` — a page that validates structurally is not a page whose Chinese has been confirmed in use, and a review by a fluent speaker has not been done.
